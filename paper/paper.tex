\title{Flying-Electron Interference Logic via Electrostatic\

\begin{abstract}
We report a full numerical study of \ent, a device architecture in which
a single-particle wavefunction is propagated through a gate-defined double-slit
barrier in a two-dimensional electron gas; a phase gate placed
behind one slit --- a shallow electrostatic lens of programmable depth ---
imparts a controlled phase shift during propagation, displacing the interference
figure at a downstream detector. The split-step solver is unitary to
$10^{-13}$ (round-off). The simulated transfer characteristic is approximately
linear with $R^2 = 0.99997$ over a gate-depth scan, with a maximum detector
imbalance of $0.44$. The architecture operates in the
\emph{single-electron coherent spatial superposition} regime: the study
propagates one delocalised coherent wave whose two-slit paths superpose at
the detector. The \emph{Entangletronica} name is therefore an architectural
label for the lens-based gate design, not a claim of multi-particle
entanglement---there is a single electron and no Bell-type resource, a point
re-emphasised in Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The flying-electron paradigm \cite{bertoni, ji, bliokh} treats the electron not as a
localised carrier but as a delocalised probability wave launched into a
ballistic channel. Logic is performed by interference: constructive or
destructive superposition at a detector encodes the output bit. Although the
device is named \emph{Entangletronica}, the physics here is strictly
single-particle: the interference is a coherent spatial superposition of a
single flying electron's two paths, and no multi-particle (entangled) degree
of freedom exists in the model. The central
engineering question is how to \emph{shape} the wave function in flight with
electrostatic gates.

\ent{} proposes that the natural tool is the \emph{electrostatic quantum lens}:
a smooth, gate-generated Gaussian potential. Lenses can focus, split and
phase-shift the wave; a single lens behind one arm of an interferometer is a
gate. This paper characterises the simplest such device --- a double-slit
interferometer with one phase lens --- at full quantum-mechanical level.

\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Wave packet and material}
\label{sec:packet}

We solve the 2D time-dependent Schr\"odinger equation
\begin{equation}
i\hbar\,\partial_t\psi(\bm r,t) = \Big[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m^*}\nabla^2 +
V(\bm r,t)\Big]\psi(\bm r,t),
\label{eq:schr}
\end{equation}
for an InGaAs 2DEG ($m^* = 0.042\,m_0$). The initial state is a
minimum-uncertainty Gaussian packet
\begin{equation}
\psi_0(\bm r) \propto \exp\!\Big[-\frac{|\bm r-\bm r_0|^2}{4s^2}
+ i k_0 (x-x_0)\Big],
\label{eq:packet}
\end{equation}
with $s = \SI{10}{nm}$, $\bm r_0 = (25,0)\,\text{nm}$, $k_0 =
\SI{0.20}{nm^{-1}}$. The central kinetic energy is
$E = \hbar^2 k_0^2/2m^* = \SI{36.3}{meV}$ and the de~Broglie wavelength
$\lambda = 2\pi/k_0 = \SI{31.4}{nm}$; the ballistic (group) velocity
$v_g = \hbar k_0/m^* \approx \SI{5.5e5}{m/s}$ carries the electron across the
\SI{130}{nm} device in roughly \SI{0.24}{ps}.

\subsection{Electrostatic lens and gate}
\label{sec:lens}

Every potential feature is a smooth Gaussian:
\begin{equation}
V(\bm r) = \sum_\ell a_\ell \exp\!\Big[-\frac{|\bm r-\bm c_\ell|^2}{2s_\ell^2}\Big],
\label{eq:lens}
\end{equation}
with $a_\ell$ the amplitude in meV. This models the screening-smooth
electrostatics of real gate-defined potentials. Three elements compose the
device (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}):
\begin{description}
\item[Entrance and exit caps] two repulsive Gaussians ($a = \SI{15}{meV}$)
at $x=0$ and $x=\SI{160}{nm}$ sit at the channel mouth and exit. Placed on the
propagation axis they act as \emph{longitudinal} entrance/exit caps; they do
\emph{not} confine the channel \emph{laterally}. The in-plane ($y$) packet is
thereby left free and is shaped only by the double-slit apertures and the
phase lens (and, at the detector, by the bin edges studied in
Sec.~\ref{sec:readout}). This corrects the earlier wording ``confine the
channel laterally.''
\item[Double-slit barrier] a repulsive Gaussian sheet at $x=\SI{60}{nm}$,
$y\in[-34,34]$\,nm, $a=\SI{12}{meV}$, with two transparent apertures of width
$s=\SI{4}{nm}$ centred at $y=\pm\SI{12}{nm}$: a coherent two-source emitter
(Young's arrangement).
\item[Phase gate] a shallow attractive lens ($a=-\SI{15}{meV}$, $s=\SI{6}{nm}$)
immediately behind the upper slit, at $(68,12)$\,nm. Its depth is
programmable through the factor $\kphi$ ($\kphi=0$: gate off).
\end{description}
The electron tunnels through the barrier and recombines downstream; the phase
lens adds $\phi(\kphi) = -\!\int_{\text{arm}} V\,dt/\hbar$ to the upper path
\emph{in flight}, displacing the interference figure.

\subsection{Numerical method}
\label{sec:solver}

Equation~\eqref{eq:schr} is advanced on a $140\times80$ grid ($\Delta x =
\SI{2}{nm}$) with the unitary split-step operator
\begin{equation}
\psi(t{+}dt) = e^{-iVdt/2}\,\mathcal F^{-1}\!\big[e^{-ik^2 dt/2}\,
\mathcal F[e^{-iVdt/2}\psi]\big],
\label{eq:split}
\end{equation}
$\hbar=m=1$, $dt=0.30$, $N_t=1300$ steps. Every run conserves the norm to
$10^{-13}$ (verified). The grid resolves $k_0$ with four-fold margin over
Nyquist.

\subsection{Free flight}
\label{sec:free}

Figure~\ref{fig:free} shows the packet at $t=0$ and after free flight:
the electron is a spreading probability wave; no trajectory exists. The
guide walls are a weak perturbation ($V_{\max}/E = 0.41$).

\subsection{Interference figure}
\label{sec:fig}

Figure~\ref{fig:interference_gate} (left) shows the final $|\psi|^2$ with the gate on.
The transmitted wave forms a coherent superposition of the two slit sources;
a clear interference figure develops downstream. The right panel superimposes
the detector-plane profile (at $x=\SI{110}{nm}$) for gate off/on: the central
fringe is displaced by the gate while the envelope is unchanged --- the
signature of a pure phase shift.

\subsection{Transfer characteristic}
\label{sec:transfer}

The detector plane is divided into two bins ($y\in(0,14)$ and
$y\in(-14,0)$\,nm). Figure~\ref{fig:transfer} shows the imbalance
\begin{equation}
\mathcal I(\kphi) = \frac{P_U - P_L}{P_U + P_L}
\label{eq:imb}
\end{equation}
vs.\ the gate-depth factor. The response is linear:
$\mathcal I = 0.083 + 0.142\,\kphi$ with $R^2 = 0.99997$
(sensitivity $\SI{0.142}{per gate unit}$); the maximum simulated imbalance is
$0.44$. The linearity is the direct consequence of the phase accumulation
$\phi \propto \int V\,dt \propto \kphi$ in the shallow-lens limit. Within
this model, ramping $\kphi$ steers the fringe across the two read-out bins.

\subsection{Fringe displacement and contrast}
\label{sec:fringe}

Figure~\ref{fig:fringe} reports the position $y_p$ of the central fringe peak
and the profile contrast $C = (\max-\min)\allowbreak/(\max+\min)$ at the detector plane.
The peak moves monotonically with $\kphi$ while the contrast remains high
($C > 0.9$), confirming that the logic output is \emph{displacement-driven}
interference, not amplitude modulation.

\subsubsection{Read-out and grid sensitivity}
\label{sec:readout}

Because the imbalance $\mathcal I$ in Eq.~(\ref{eq:imb}) is a functional of the
detector profile, we separated the numerical stability of the \emph{wave
dynamics} from that of the \emph{read-out functional}
(Figure~\ref{fig:readout}; \texttt{scripts/\allowbreak readout\_sensitivity.py}). For the
same propagated state, the fringe peak, centroid and width are stable under
grid refinement $\Delta x = \{4,2,1,0.5\}$\,nm and the profile $L^2$ error
decays near second order; the two-bin imbalance read as a continuous functional
of the interpolated profile is stable at $\approx 0.154$ already at $\Delta x =
2$\,nm. Read instead as a box-sum on the raw grid, the same imbalance drifts
($\approx 0.31\rightarrow 0.23\rightarrow 0.20\rightarrow 0.17$). The apparent
non-convergence of the earlier two-bin imbalance is therefore an artefact of
the discretised read-out, not of the underlying dynamics --- the solver,
geometry and read-out are three distinct objects.

Formally, denote by $P_{\Delta x}(y)=|\psi(x_d,y)|^2$ the detector profile
computed on a grid of pitch $\Delta x$. The 2D wave dynamics converge under
refinement: the profile $L^2$ error
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:L2err}
L_2(\Delta x)\;=\;\Big[\int\!\big(P_{\Delta x}(y)-P_{0.5\,\mathrm{nm}}(y)\big)^{2}\,dy\Big]^{1/2},
\end{equation}
decays $\propto(\Delta x)^{2}$ (near second order) as
$\Delta x\to0.5\,\mathrm{nm}$, with the fringe peak, centroid and width already
converged at $\Delta x=\SI{2}{nm}$. The \emph{read-out} is a separate object:
read as a continuous functional of this same profile the imbalance is stable
at $\approx0.154$ from $\Delta x=\SI{2}{nm}$ down; read instead as a raw
lattice box-sum it drifts $0.31\to0.23\to0.20\to0.17$. That drift is an
\emph{artefact of the discrete summation}, not of the convergent wave dynamics.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:disc}

The double-slit geometry is the simplest building block discussed here: the
phase-gate parameter $\kphi$ shifts the interference read-out (a physical
gate-voltage transfer is beyond the present model). The read-out analysis of
Sec.~\ref{sec:readout} also draws the methodological line between the
simulated phenomenon and the metric chosen to observe it. Two features stand out. First, the linearity of Eq.~\eqref{eq:imb} means the
output is a faithful analogue of the gate waveform in the shallow-lens regime; in
the two-bin read-out it becomes a threshold logic level. Second, because the
electron is flying, the same gate could in principle be modulated \emph{during}
the transit, suggesting in-flight interference control (see~\cite{ji} for the
experimental platform; decoherence-limited coherence length is probed by
Mach--Zehnder interferometry in \cite{roulleau}). The two propagation times quoted here measure
different endpoints and are not in conflict. $T=\SI{390}{solver\,units}=\SI{0.14}{ps}$
is the \emph{simulated} window: at the group velocity
$v_g=\SI{5.5e5}{m/s}$ it carries the packet centre from
$x=\SI{25}{nm}$ (source) to $x\approx\SI{103}{nm}$, inside the
$\sigma=\SI{10}{nm}$ width and approaching the detector plane at
$x=\SI{110}{nm}$. The \SI{0.24}{ps} figure, in contrast, is the full
source-to-channel-end transit over $\approx\SI{130}{nm}$; it is quoted only to
set the in-flight modulation bandwidth and is not the simulated window. The
relevant distance is therefore source-to-detector ($\approx\SI{85}{nm}$), not
the channel length.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:concl}

We have demonstrated numerically that a single-particle wavefunction, shaped by
idealized electrostatic lenses, can be propagated through a Young interferometer
such that a gate lens behind one aperture displaces the interference figure
monotonically ($R^2 = 0.99997$ over the fitted regime) up to an imbalance of
0.44, with norm conserved to round-off in the discrete split-step scheme. The
architecture propagates a probability wave rather than localising it in a
qubit. Extending beyond this idealised single-particle model --- including
thermal spread, disorder and dephasing --- is required before device-level claims
can be made. For the observables considered here, the wave dynamics and the
continuous detector read-out are numerically stable at $\Delta x = 2$\,nm
(Sec.~\ref{sec:readout}). Next steps are the physical gate-voltage mapping
($\kphi \rightarrow V_g \rightarrow V(x,y)$, beyond the present model) and
many-body or dephasing studies.
